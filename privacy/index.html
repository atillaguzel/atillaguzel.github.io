Privacy

Last changed 19 November 2026

This site counts its visits. I'd like to know which pages get read and which ones nobody finds, and that is all the counting is for. This page says what is counted, who does the counting and how to turn it off.

counting

The switch needs JavaScript. With JavaScript off, as it is now, nothing is counted.

What is counted

  • The page you opened, and the page or site that sent you to it.
  • Whether you reached the end of a page, and the links you follow away from the site.
  • A few button presses: sharing a post, using one of the small demos, copying a block of code, switching the theme.
  • The kind of device and browser you use, and the language it is set to.
  • The country or region you appear to be in. It is worked out from your internet address, and the address itself is not kept.
  • A random number, kept in a cookie, so that two pages read one after the other count as one visit.

What is not

  • Anything you type. The demos run in your browser, and what you enter into them stays there.
  • Your name, your email or an account. The site has no sign-in and nothing to fill in and send.
  • Anything for advertising. No ad network is involved, and Google's advertising features are switched off for this site.

I look at totals, such as how many times a post was opened in a week. I don't follow what one visitor does.

Who does the counting

Google Analytics, a service run by Google. It works on my behalf here: Google handles these numbers on my instructions, and I have switched off the settings that would let it use them for its own products. Google describes its side in how Google uses information from sites that use its services. Its servers may be outside the UK.

The detail behind each visit is kept for two months, the shortest period Google offers. What is tied to the random number in your cookie, such as the date of your first visit, is kept for up to 14 months after your last one. After that only the totals remain.

That is the whole list. The pages, styles, scripts and fonts all come from this site. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub, like any host, sees the internet address each request comes from. GitHub's privacy statement covers that.

Turning it off

  • Press the switch at the top of this page. Counting stops at once and the Google cookies are deleted. The choice is kept in this browser, so another browser or device needs its own.
  • If your browser sends the Global Privacy Control signal, the site takes that as a no and never starts.
  • If your device is set to a European time zone outside the UK, the site asks first and counts nothing until you say yes, because the rules there are different.

Your rights, and how to reach me

I'm Atilla Guzel. I live in London and I am responsible for this site. I count visits because I have what the law calls a legitimate interest in knowing whether the site is read. UK data protection law gives you the right to object to that, which the switch does, and to ask me what is held about you or to have it deleted.

The way to reach me is a message on LinkedIn. If you think I have got this wrong, you can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.
